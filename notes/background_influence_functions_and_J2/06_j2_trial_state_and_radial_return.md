# Elastic trial state and radial-return derivation

## Purpose and route

Lecture 5 specifies continuous plastic evolution. Lecture 4, however, needs a finite-step stress update for every supplied strain. We use backward Euler to evaluate plastic flow at the unknown end state, an elastic predictor to decide whether flow is needed, and radial-return geometry to reduce the active update to one scalar consistency equation. Linear hardening makes that scalar equation solvable in closed form.

This lecture derives the update; Lecture 7 collects its branches and inserts it into the global Newton loop. A local update is evaluated at fixed supplied strain. Changing that strain is the job of the outer equilibrium solver.

## Assumptions, notation, and known state

Use 3D small strain, isotropic elasticity, associated von Mises plasticity, and linear isotropic hardening. The bulk modulus $K_{\mathrm b}>0$, shear modulus $G>0$, hardening modulus $H\geq0$, and initial yield stress $\sigma_{\mathrm y0}>0$ have stress units. $\mathbb C$ is the fourth-order elastic tensor. Stress and strain symbols are symmetric tensors; $\mathbf I_3$ is the identity tensor, $\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf a)=\sum_i a_{ii}$, $\mathbf a:\mathbf b=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}b_{ij}$, and $\operatorname{dev}(\mathbf a)=\mathbf a-\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf a)\mathbf I_3$.

| Quantity | Role in this local call | Units |
|---|---|---|
| $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{n+1}$ | Supplied total strain, held fixed during correction | Dimensionless |
| $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm p}$, $p_n$ | Committed plastic strain and accumulated history | Dimensionless; $p_n\geq0$ |
| $\boldsymbol\sigma^{\mathrm{tr}}$, $\mathbf s^{\mathrm{tr}}$ | Trial stress and its deviatoric part | Stress |
| $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{mean}}$ | Equivalent and mean stress | Stress |
| $f^{\mathrm{tr}}$ | Trial yield-function value | Stress |
| $\Delta\lambda$ | Nonnegative plastic multiplier increment | Dimensionless |

Subscripts $n,n+1$ denote load-step endpoints. The superscript $\mathrm{tr}$ always refers to the predictor for the current step $n+1$; that subscript is omitted for readability. During global Newton iteration, the supplied strain is the current iterate's strain and all returned step-$n+1$ quantities are provisional. The old history stays fixed.

The elastic law is $\boldsymbol\sigma=\mathbb C:(\boldsymbol\varepsilon-\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm p})$. The equivalent stress is $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}=\sqrt{\tfrac32\mathbf s:\mathbf s}$, where $\mathbf s=\operatorname{dev}(\boldsymbol\sigma)$. Yielding is governed by $f=\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}-(\sigma_{\mathrm y0}+Hp)$. Strain is dimensionless. The FEM conversion uses engineering shear in order $(11,22,33,12,23,13)$; the tensor contractions here must not be replaced by unweighted Voigt norms.

## 1. Choose an endpoint integration rule

The continuous law is $\dot{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}^{\mathrm p}=\dot\lambda\tfrac32\mathbf s/\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}$. Backward Euler evaluates its direction at the end of the step. This is a numerical approximation to the path evolution; it makes the active correction consistent with the updated surface at that endpoint.

**Equation (RR-B1) - Backward-Euler internal-state update**

$$
\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{n+1}^{\mathrm p}
=\boldsymbol\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm p}+\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm p},
\qquad
p_{n+1}=p_n+\Delta\lambda
$$

Here $\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm p}$ and $\Delta\lambda$ are load-step increments. The endpoint direction will be specified in Eq. (RR-6). The continuous loading conditions become endpoint complementarity for this update.

**Equation (RR-B2) - Discrete loading conditions**

$$
f_{n+1}\leq0,\qquad \Delta\lambda\geq0,\qquad \Delta\lambda f_{n+1}=0
$$

The elastic branch has zero plastic increment; the active branch must end at $f_{n+1}=0$. Endpoint consistency does not make a finite step exact for every loading path: load-step resolution still affects accuracy.

## 2. Test whether the step can remain elastic

Before solving for new plastic flow, freeze the old history and compute the stress that would result if the supplied strain were accommodated elastically.

**Equation (RR-1) - Elastic trial stress**

$$
\boldsymbol\sigma^{\mathrm{tr}}
=\mathbb C:(\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{n+1}-\boldsymbol\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm p})
$$

To test admissibility, take $\mathbf s^{\mathrm{tr}}=\operatorname{dev}(\boldsymbol\sigma^{\mathrm{tr}})$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{tr}}=\sqrt{\tfrac32\mathbf s^{\mathrm{tr}}:\mathbf s^{\mathrm{tr}}}$. Compare it with the strength generated by the old accumulated history.

**Equation (RR-2) - Trial yield check**

$$
f^{\mathrm{tr}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{tr}}-(\sigma_{\mathrm y0}+Hp_n)
$$

If $f^{\mathrm{tr}}\leq0$, accept the trial stress and leave both internal variables unchanged. This includes elastic loading, unloading, or reloading; the old state having yielded does not force the next step to be plastic. If $f^{\mathrm{tr}}>0$, the elastic assumption fails and the following active correction is required.

## 3. Derive the elastic stress relaxation

Why does plastic correction contain $2G$? Isotropic elasticity maps a symmetric elastic strain $\mathbf a$ to $K_{\mathrm b}\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf a)\mathbf I_3+2G\operatorname{dev}(\mathbf a)$. Its deviatoric part is therefore

**Equation (RR-3) - Deviatoric isotropic elasticity**

$$
\mathbf s=2G\operatorname{dev}(\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm e})
$$

The trial elastic strain is $\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm e,tr}=\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{n+1}-\boldsymbol\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm p}$. At the same supplied total strain, replacing old plastic strain by its updated value reduces elastic strain by the plastic increment.

**Equation (RR-4) - Trial-to-corrected elastic-strain difference**

$$
\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{n+1}^{\mathrm e}-\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm e,tr}
=-\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm p}
$$

This is not the actual elastic load-step increment, which would also include the total-strain change from step $n$. Since the plastic increment is traceless, the volumetric term in isotropic elasticity contributes no correction. Subtracting the two stresses gives

**Equation (RR-5) - Deviatoric stress correction**

$$
\mathbf s_{n+1}=\mathbf s^{\mathrm{tr}}-2G\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm p}
$$

Thus $2G$ follows from the full deviatoric elastic law, not just a shear-component convention. In engineering-shear matrix form the shear entries of the elastic matrix are $G$ because the strain entries are doubled. The hydrostatic stress is unchanged during the correction because the plastic increment has zero trace.

## 4. Backward Euler makes the return radial

Evaluate the associated von Mises direction at the endpoint, as required by the chosen integration scheme.

**Equation (RR-6) - Endpoint-direction plastic increment**

$$
\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm p}
=\Delta\lambda\frac32\frac{\mathbf s_{n+1}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{eq},n+1}}
$$

Insert Eq. (RR-6) into Eq. (RR-5) and collect the terms multiplying the corrected deviatoric stress. This step reveals why the tensor problem has only one unknown scalar magnitude.

**Equation (RR-6a) - Collinearity of trial and corrected stress**

$$
\left(1+\frac{3G\Delta\lambda}{\sigma_{\mathrm{eq},n+1}}\right)\mathbf s_{n+1}
=\mathbf s^{\mathrm{tr}}
$$

On an active endpoint the equivalent stress equals the positive yield strength. The prefactor is therefore positive: the corrected stress has the same direction as the trial stress, not its opposite. Since equivalent stress is proportional to the tensor norm, normalization removes this positive factor.

**Equation (RR-6b) - Equal normalized directions**

$$
\frac{\mathbf s_{n+1}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{eq},n+1}}
=\frac{\mathbf s^{\mathrm{tr}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{tr}}}
$$

Consequently the unknown endpoint direction can be evaluated from the known trial state. This is a derived property of this isotropic associated model, not an explicit integration approximation.

**Equation (RR-7) - Trial-direction plastic increment**

$$
\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm p}
=\Delta\lambda\frac32\frac{\mathbf s^{\mathrm{tr}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{tr}}}
$$

## 5. Consistency determines the plastic amount

Substitute Eq. (RR-7) into the stress correction to isolate its scalar radial factor.

**Equation (RR-8) - Corrected deviatoric stress**

$$
\mathbf s_{n+1}
=\left(1-\frac{3G\Delta\lambda}{\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{tr}}}\right)\mathbf s^{\mathrm{tr}}
$$

Alternatively, take the equivalent-stress norm of Eq. (RR-6a). Its positive prefactor can be taken outside the norm, giving the equivalent-stress reduction without losing a sign.

**Equation (RR-9) - Scalar stress reduction**

$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq},n+1}+3G\Delta\lambda=\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{tr}}
$$

The same plastic amount advances accumulated history under the chosen normalization.

**Equation (RR-10) - Accumulated-history update**

$$
p_{n+1}=p_n+\Delta\lambda
$$

Insert this history into the hardening law to obtain the strength the corrected stress must meet.

**Equation (RR-11) - Updated yield strength**

$$
\sigma_{\mathrm y,n+1}=\sigma_{\mathrm y0}+H(p_n+\Delta\lambda)
$$

Because the active branch has $\Delta\lambda>0$, Eq. (RR-B2) requires equality of equivalent stress and updated strength. Combine the preceding two effects in one scalar equation.

**Equation (RR-12) - Discrete consistency equation**

$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{tr}}-3G\Delta\lambda
=\sigma_{\mathrm y0}+H(p_n+\Delta\lambda)
$$

Move the terms containing $\Delta\lambda$ to one side. The remaining difference is exactly the trial overstress.

**Equation (RR-12a) - Collection of multiplier terms**

$$
(3G+H)\Delta\lambda
=\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{tr}}-(\sigma_{\mathrm y0}+Hp_n)=f^{\mathrm{tr}}
$$

Since $3G+H>0$, division gives the active-branch multiplier.

**Equation (RR-13) - Closed-form plastic multiplier**

$$
\boxed{\Delta\lambda=\frac{f^{\mathrm{tr}}}{3G+H}}
\qquad\text{for }f^{\mathrm{tr}}>0
$$

The numerator is positive overstress. The denominator combines elastic stress relaxation, $3G$, and strength increase, $H$. Both have stress units, so the multiplier is dimensionless and nonnegative. This local scalar equation needs no iterative solve for linear hardening. Global equilibrium may still require many Newton iterations; a more general hardening law can also require a local scalar solve.

## Checks and next question

The returned strength is positive, so Eq. (RR-9) implies that the radial factor in Eq. (RR-8) is positive. On the active branch $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{tr}}>0$, so trial normalization is safe. In the perfect-plastic limit $H=0$, the multiplier is $f^{\mathrm{tr}}/(3G)$; as overstress tends to zero from above, the plastic increment vanishes continuously. Hydrostatic stress is unchanged, and $\operatorname{tr}(\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\mathrm p})=0$ follows directly from Eq. (RR-7).

**Checkpoint:** Why does hardening enter the denominator together with elastic stress relaxation, and why does a closed-form local multiplier not remove the need for global Newton iteration?
